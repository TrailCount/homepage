Understanding our footprint in wild places.

A project of Adirondack Wilderness Advocates

TrailCount builds tools for exploring wild land, made for the people who care for it — at a public agency or a private land trust. Explore how many people use a trail and when, how far a place sits from the nearest road, who holds the land around it, and how steep the ground is. Good stewardship rests on evidence like this, and on a map it is easy to read — so decisions follow the land, not anecdote. The work begins in New York’s Adirondack Park.

  • Measure unobtrusively. Battery-powered counters at the trailhead log each passing hiker and report in daily over cellular — no cameras, no wires, nothing asked of visitors.
  • Explore the whole landscape. Visitor counts are one layer. Remoteness, private ownership, and trail grade are others, each mapped from public data.
  • Protect what is pristine. Crowded trails and shrinking remote core show up early, so stewardship — trail hardening, education, rerouting, protection — goes where the land needs it first.
