Understanding our footprint in wild places.

TrailCount builds data tools for the people who care for wild land, whether they work for a public agency or a private land trust. Good stewardship rests on evidence: how many people use a trail and when, how steep the ground is, how far a place sits from the nearest road, who holds the land around it. We gather that evidence, put it on a map, and make it easy to read — so decisions follow the land, not anecdote. The work begins in New York’s Adirondack Park.

  • Measure unobtrusively. Battery-powered counters at the trailhead log each passing hiker and report in daily over cellular — no cameras, no wires, nothing asked of visitors.
  • See the whole landscape. Visitor counts are one layer. Trail grades, distance from motorized access, and the pattern of public and private ownership are others, mapped from public data.
  • Protect what is pristine. Crowded trails and shrinking remote core show up early, so stewardship — trail hardening, education, rerouting, protection — goes where the land needs it first.

TrailCount is sponsored by Adirondack Wilderness Advocates and built in collaboration with RIT senior-project teams.

Maps of the Adirondack Park

Open to anyone — no sign-in needed to explore.

Remoteness Explorer → How far each acre of the Park is from the nearest road, ATV trail, snowmobile trail, or motorized water. Close a road or draw a new one and watch remote core grow or fragment.

Steepness Explorer → DEC hiking trails colored by grade, computed from USGS elevation data. Filter by unit and trail type, or hide everything gentler than a grade you choose.

Private Land Explorer → The other half of the Park: who holds the large private landholdings, grouped by parent company, with APA land class, conservation easements, and forest-tax commitments.
